# Discovery Submission and Credit Preservation

**Operational protocol (founding version).** This helps maintain a reliable evidence trail; it cannot prevent someone outside the initiative from making a false claim, nor determine patent rights.

## Before submitting publicly

1. Search [Discussions](https://github.com/rajivamin/multa-lumina/discussions), [Issues](https://github.com/rajivamin/multa-lumina/issues), and the [Discovery Ledger](../DISCOVERY_LEDGER.md) for related ideas.
2. Identify who contributed **which** part: question, conceptual insight, software, investigation, data, validation, supervision, writing, and later improvements. Prefer links over unsupported assertions of priority.
3. For evidence you are ready to share, keep original files, configuration, timestamps, code commit IDs, and any independent records. Do not publicize employer secrets, household footage, credentials, or personal data.
4. **Patent-sensitive or confidential work:** Consult qualified counsel or your employer's authorized representative *before* public disclosure. Publishing or licensing material can have irreversible consequences. We cannot accept confidential deposits or guarantee a confidential timestamping service.
5. If relevant, create a SHA-256 digest of the complete, original research package and preserve an independently timestamped receipt. **A digest alone without a trusted time record is not proof of when it existed.** An embargo may protect confidentiality but also delays community inspection and independent verification.

## Submit your finding

1. Open a pull request with a Markdown record based on [the research template](../research/RECORD_TEMPLATE.md), or an Issue/Discussion pointing to the record. No claim must be treated as validated merely because someone posted it.
2. Include exact claim, research track, system configuration and versions, methods and reproducibility steps, costs where relevant, artifacts and evidence links, limitations, prior work, and contributor roles.
3. Link every predecessor or dependent study and disclose material human and AI assistance.
4. In the pull request, explain exactly what changed and which collaborators should be credited.
5. An independent reviewer assesses the claim and assigns a precise status: **reported**, **reproduced**, **validated**, **disputed**, or **superseded**. Not every proposed claim needs independent replication before it can be discussed; validation requires sufficient independent evidence.
6. Accepted records are indexed in the [Discovery Ledger](../DISCOVERY_LEDGER.md). Corrections are linked rather than silently overwriting the history.

## If someone misattributes your work

- Preserve your own copies, dated links, and explanation of the specific disputed contribution.
- Do **not** post accusations, sensitive documents, or personal information publicly.
- The initiative needs a designated private reporting contact and independent review/appeal workflow before publicly soliciting sensitive disclosures. This is currently outstanding and tracked in [governance issue #8](https://github.com/rajivamin/multa-lumina/issues/8).
- Maintainers should acknowledge receipt privately, offer all affected parties an opportunity to supply evidence, document their reasoning, and publish a proportionate correction when appropriate. They cannot adjudicate legal title or prevent external claims.

## Archiving roadmap

Public GitHub commits and accepted records are the initial working evidence trail. For milestone research releases, the initiative plans independent archival preservation, for example Zenodo DOI-backed release snapshots. That service has **not** been configured yet.

## What this does *not* do

A timestamp doesn't prove originality. A DOI doesn't prove correctness. A merged PR doesn't grant paper authorship. The Apache 2.0 license is not a nondisclosure agreement or an anti-plagiarism enforcement system. A complete test pass requires the separate official evaluation protocols.
